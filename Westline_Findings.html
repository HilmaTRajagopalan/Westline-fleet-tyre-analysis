1. Source audit (done before any analysis)

1a. Exports overlap; one mislabels its vehicle measured

Each June file runs to ~06:00 on 1 Jul and each July file starts at 18:00 on 30 Jun, so 12 hours are present twice (2,869 rows). All overlapping rows are identical, so de-duplicating on (vehicle, time) loses nothing. T1's July file carries KA25B4471 on every row (27,082 rows); a join on the Vehicle column would silently drop it, so I key vehicles on the file name. After de-duplication: 556,310 rows.

1b. Data-quality facts that shape the method measured

  • Rows are not evenly spaced: ~18 s while active, ~2 min parked (100% of rows on 0-km days). Every summary uses fixed 5-10 min bins, not row means.
  • Receiver clock and GPS time never differ by more than 3 s.
  • GPS coverage is poor only on T1 (83%) and T2 (86%) against ~98% elsewhere; gaps are in daytime. This is the likely source of the manager's "map jumps around".
  • Sensor dropouts hit ~16% of records, always as a whole sensor (PSI, TEMP and BATT together); baseline ~1.2% per slot.
  • Avg Battery is the mean of sensors that reported, so it moves when a sensor drops out; not used.

1c. The odometer log is the most trustworthy source measured

Opening and closing odometers chain with no breaks, KM Run equals closing minus opening, and GPS distance agrees within 2-6% on every truck (max daily deviation 3.5%). Used as ground truth for "was the truck moving today". Caveat: it is daily, so it cannot say when in the day the truck ran.

1d. Position systems measured

C = left and D = right. The Data Dictionary's parentheticals are reversed (it says "C = co-driver side (right)" but also "CF1 = front left"). Tyre serials embed the original side: C matches L in 34 of 34 non-rotated rows, D matches R in 34 of 34. Anyone following the dictionary would flip every left/right tread comparison. The T1 rotation is real and is the Goa truck, not the Karwar truck as the manager guessed: 3LO/3RO carry each other's serials, re-fitted 28 Jun (a Sunday). The register is a 15 Jul snapshot: the failed T2 5RI tyre (serial ...-0242) was overwritten by its replacement (...-0901, 12 Jul).

1e. TPMS confirms the register events and finds no others measured

On 28 Jun the batteries of T1's 3LO and 3RO slots exchange values (62 and 87): sensors travelled with the tyres. On 12 Jul T2's 5RI battery jumps 48 to 98: new sensor. No other battery step in six weeks, so no undocumented tyre or sensor change. A slot is a position, not a tyre, so per-tyre histories are rebuilt through serials.

2. Physics first: pressure follows temperature

A sealed tyre is nearly constant-volume gas, so pressure rises and falls with gas temperature; a 100 PSI cold tyre reads ~115 PSI after hard running with no air added. A fixed raw-PSI alarm therefore mixes air quantity with temperature. Test: on 0-km days (Sundays) the slope of PSI against sensor C should be ~0.38-0.43 PSI/C.

  • measured 490 parked tyre-days: median slope 0.713 PSI/C (IQR 0.690-0.737, median r = 0.97), 1.86x the gas-law value, identical on every axle and truck. The slope does not scale with the pressure level, which a gas-law effect would.
  • hypothesis The reported temperature is the valve-stem sensor body, which swings about half as much as the gas inside. Consequence (solid): textbook compensation would under-correct by almost half, so I use the empirical 0.71 PSI/C, referred to 25 C.
  • derived Within-day 5-95% range over 3,780 tyre-days: raw PSI median 13.6 (90th percentile 20.2) against compensated median 1.5 (90th percentile 1.8).

Confidence: high on the empirical slope. Would change my mind: the vendor documenting the probe location, or a reference gauge and IR reading on a parked truck.

3. "Fourteen alarms every afternoon": the Goa truck

On a Goa day the truck leaves Belagavi ~06:30, descends the Anmod ghat (drive-axle tyres ~70 C by 08:30 while steer and axles 2-3 stay at 40-50 C), parks in Goa until ~14:45, then all tyres lose 15-20 C in half an hour as it leaves, and pressure falls 10-20 PSI with it. That is a thermal event; pressure recovers as tyres warm on the climb.

  • measured On 10 dates, 4-6 of 6 trucks on different roads show a cooling of 8 C or more in 30 minutes within the same 20-minute window. A puncture cannot do that to six trucks. First events 10-11 Jun match the manager's "rain came early, second week of June".
  • measured Not literally every afternoon: 17 of 45 days (10 of 30 in June) have an all-tyre afternoon cooling event on T1.
  • hypothesis Rain as the cause: no weather data in the pack. Residual puzzle: timing agrees to the same 10-minute bin across trucks 100+ km apart, more uniform than real showers; a shared pipeline component cannot be excluded.

4. How the depots inflate, and why Hubballi "always reads low"

The standard is a cold pressure (before running, or after 3 h standing). Filling to the card number while hot leaves the tyre below the card after cooling by about 0.71 x (hot minus 25 C), i.e. 8-17 PSI at 35-50 C. Releasing air from a hot tyre that reads above the card is the same error reversed. Method: detect depot sessions as a temperature-referred step of 3.5 PSI or more within 20 minutes with under 2 C temperature change, cluster per truck, compare readings before and after with the card.

  • measured Frequency: truck-wide sessions every ~2-5 weeks per truck, not "ten-fifteen days".
  • measured No difference in leakage: median -1.55 PSI/30 d at Hubballi vs -1.36 at Belagavi (Mann-Whitney p = 0.24), inside the manufacturer's ~3 PSI/month allowance. The manager's "Hubballi leaks" is the hot-fill artefact misread.
  • The spec sheet itself says visual inspection cannot detect 10-15% under-inflation, and running well under standard overheats the casing and shortens life. Nothing in the tread sheets shows it yet; six weeks is too short.

Confidence: high on the hot-fill mechanism (it explains sign, size, timing and the manager's own quoted numbers). Would change my mind: a depot log showing fills were done cold, or TPMS reading low only in the afternoon.

6. "Which tyre will let me down?" A watch-list

Two routes: a tyre loses air much faster than its truck-mates, or its sensor is dying. Both use temperature-referred pressure, compare each tyre with the rest of its own truck, handle the T1 swap and ignore single-tyre top-ups.

Confidence: high that these tyres deviate from neighbours; moderate on monthly rates (short window, top-ups interrupt the series).

7. A better alert, and an honest test of it

  • Fast-loss rule: a tyre 4 PSI or more below its own previous 6 hours and 4 PSI or more worse than the truck's median tyre change, sustained 30 minutes.
  • Slow-leak rule: nightly gap-to-truck-median falling faster than 6 PSI/month (2x the allowance).

measured Backtest over 45 days x 84 tyres: the fast-loss rule fires 4 times. It fires on T2 5RI at 03:40 on 11 Jul (~8.5 h before the roadside stop) and three more times on the afternoons depots released air from hot tyres (T3 5 Jun, T5 8 Jun): real pressure losses caused by people, suppressible by recognising a truck-wide depot session or by fixing the cold-fill rule.

Limits. I chose thresholds after seeing this failure and the noise, so the near-zero alert count is partly tuned. One failure, one truck, six weeks: no detection rate or false-alarm rate can be quoted. "One day's notice" is not available for sudden failures (hours); for slow leaks it is (weeks at the measured rates).

8. Tread depth

Tread loss needs the same tyre measured twice, the km in between, and a gauge finer than the change. The pack has two inspections for T1, one each for T2, T3, T4, none for T5, T6.

  • measured Right tyres are deeper than left on 20 of 20 axle comparisons (median +2.6 mm). On T1 (5,074 km between inspections) left tyres lost ~0.15 mm per 1,000 km vs ~0.08 on the right. The asymmetry is fleet-wide, not one tyre. A road-edge or camber explanation fits left-hand traffic but cannot be tested here (hypothesis).
  • measured No inflation wear pattern yet: shoulder minus centre tread sd 0.11 mm, max 0.25 mm, against 0.1 mm gauge resolution. T3, ~15% under for weeks, shows nothing. Do not read this as "under-inflation is harmless".
  • T1 wear is slow (0.07-0.16 mm per 1,000 km), limited to about +/-30% by gauge and +/-1 day of km. At ~146 km/day, tyres at ~10-14 mm have well over a year of tread.
  • "Are Hubballi roads eating my tyres?" cannot be answered: no Hubballi truck has two inspections; tyre ages, brands and fitting dates differ; km before fitting is unknown. Pressure leakage is the wrong instrument.

9. The ghat and braking

  • measured Goa drive-axle tyres hit ~67-71 C around 08:30 on every Anmod descent (mean 69.2 C, range 67.3-70.9) while steer and axles 2-3 stay ~42-50 C; raw pressure peaks at ~130 PSI.
  • measured No upward trend: -0.8 C per 30 days (p = 0.19, n = 19). The driver's "pedal going soft" is a feel that a stem-temperature trace cannot test.
  • T2 runs hotter than T1 on alternate days (mean 75 C vs 69 C) and cooler on the others (52 C). The failure day was a cool day (peak 55 C), so heat is not an obvious cause of the failure.
  • Not available: descent speed, gear, pedal pressure, load. The manager says the box records speed and altitude, but they are not exported.

11. Five questions for Mr Vijayan, in order of importance

  1. At Hubballi, exactly when and on what reading do staff add or release air, and do they ever release air from a hot tyre reading above the card? Why: hot fills and two hot releases leave T3/T4/T5 7-17 PSI under standard when cold. Action: if confirmed, replace the routine with cold-only fills, re-check the three trucks next morning, and drop the "Hubballi leaks" idea.
  2. What alert rules and thresholds did TyreSense configure, who receives them, and did anyone get an alert on the Karwar truck between 03:15 and 12:00 on 11 July? Why: the pack has no alert rules; this separates "alert never existed" from "ignored in thermal noise". Action: route a single-tyre relative-pressure alert to the driver.
  3. For failed tyre ...5RI-0242: when was it fitted, new or retread, which brand, and what did the casing show? Why: the register overwrote it and the inspection shows a 3.9 mm mismatch with identical readings. Action: decide on a twin-matching rule and re-measure other twins.
  4. What does each truck carry by direction (empty, part, full)? Why: the card is for rated axle load; heat, pressure need and the Goa/Karwar difference cannot be separated from load. Action: add a three-level load tick to the daily km log.
  5. Can the inspector re-inspect all six trucks at 5,000 km or more intervals, and have any tyres been rotated or replaced since 15 July? Why: only T1 has a wear rate. Action: a second set on T3/T4 and a first on T5/T6 answers the Hubballi roads question with data.

12. What this pack cannot answer

  • Whether Hubballi roads wear tyres faster (one inspection per Hubballi truck).
  • What the vendor's alerts do (rules not provided; statements are inference).
  • Whether the sensor temperature is gas or stem temperature (hypothesis only).
  • Load, speed, altitude, brake state, weather (not recorded or not exported).
  • Why 5RI failed, and whether failures generally have a slow precursor (one failure).
  • Whether the depot gauge is accurate in absolute terms (it agrees with TPMS, but both could share an offset; a calibrated reference gauge would settle it).
  • Sensor battery life (six weeks of a roughly linear fit).
  • Financial impact, fleet-wide prevalence, events during sensor sleep or missing exports, and whether a tyre was truly cold at each reading.

14. Method notes: beliefs I had to drop

  • "T6 is over-inflated by ~10 PSI": an artefact of raw (hot) medians; referred to 25 C, T6 sits on the card.
  • "A fixed 90% low threshold explains the Goa afternoon alarms": false; that rule is silent on Goa, a rapid-fall rule reproduces them.
  • "The T3/T4 step down on 5-6 Jun is a sensor glitch": it is a hot release at 15:30-16:10, the opposite of a fill, at the same hour as the fills.
  • "Leak rates differ by depot": they do not (p = 0.24); the outliers are individual tyres.
  • "The textbook gas law will compensate temperature": it under-corrects by about half for this sensor.
  • I did not treat the vendor's thresholds as known, the weather as known, or the TPMS temperature as the gas temperature.
