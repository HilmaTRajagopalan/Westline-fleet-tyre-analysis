# Fleet tyre programme operational and management problems (India focus, small/mid fleets)

Method note: about 11 searches and 1 successful page fetch. Most search results returned summaries, not full pages, and only the ATMA/ITTAC article was fetched in full. Several claims are therefore from secondary or vendor sources and are flagged. No primary India-specific survey of truck-fleet inflation-check compliance was found.

## 1. Tyre share of operating cost; fuel and wear cost of under-inflation; share of fleets with proper inflation checks

### Takeaway
Indian sources give conflicting tyre shares of operating cost, from about 7% to 30%, all from vendor or trade press, with no rigorous primary study. Under-inflation is endemic: ATMA/ITTAC found 53% of Indian passenger-vehicle tyres at least 10% low, and US truck data (FMCSA/EPA) show roughly half or more of truck tyres are off-pressure. Fuel penalties per 10 psi are small in percentage terms (about 0.2-1%) but tyre-life losses (about 9-16%) are larger.

### Cited Findings
- Tyre share of Indian fleet cost, claims conflict: 20-30% of total cost, second after fuel (fleet-software blog) — [FleetX](https://blog.fleetx.io/how-to-manage-tyre-operations-inventory-effectively); about 10% with fuel about 70% — [Pulse/ETAuto](https://www.pulse.bot/transportation/news/indias-energy-vulnerability-has-a-tyre-problem-f0c969bf-ad3c-49e1-a6e6-a3fd78253e64/); about 7% of truck operating cost — [FleetX tyre management](https://www.fleetx.io/tyre-management) (vendor); 12-15% of transporter expenses (JK Tyre piece) and "almost 30%" (Tyre Trends) — [Tyre Trends](https://tyre-trends.com/spotlight/retreading-reimagined) and [Motor India/JK Tyre](https://www.motorindiaonline.in/?p=39883).
- Fuel share of fleet operating cost in India quoted at 30-40% (tracking vendor) to as much as 60% (Michelin launch remark) — [Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/michelin-launches-x-multi-energy-z2-truck-and-bus-tyre-in-india-135565).
- ATMA/ITTAC survey (reported July 2026): more than 130,000 passenger-vehicle tyres (including two-wheelers) inspected; 32% were more than 20% below recommended pressure and another 21% were 10-20% below (53% total). Estimated waste is about 425.7 million litres of petrol a year, over INR 45 billion (INR 4,500 crore), about 0.75% of India's vehicle fuel use. Fieldwork dates not stated ("past few years"). — [Tyre Trends](https://tyre-trends.com/auto-trends/low-tyre-pressure-costs-india-inr-45-bln-in-wasted-petrol-each-year-says-atma-survey); also [Daily Pioneer](https://dailypioneer.com/news/low-tyre-pressure-costs-india-rs-4500-cr-in-fuel-every-year-atma)
- Older Apollo survey (about 2010; 36,000 tyres, 9,000 cars, 50 cities): only 22% at OE-recommended pressure, about 44% over-inflated, 34% under-inflated. Passenger cars only, and dated. — [Tyrepress](https://tyrepress.com/2010/01/apollo-registers-tyre-pressure-safety-concerns-on-indian-market); [Tire Review](https://www.tirereview.com/?p=70378)
- Note the Apollo result: over-inflation was more common than under-inflation, so "wrong pressure" is not only a low-pressure problem.
- US FMCSA-derived figures (secondary summaries, figures conflict): 55% of commercial vehicles have at least one tyre 10+ psi low; 1 in 5 tractors has a tyre 20+ psi low; only about 50% of tyres within 5% of target (2003 roadside survey), or 28% properly inflated in an earlier study. Fuel penalty for 10 psi under-inflation quoted from 0.2% to about 1% (some DOE-attributed sources 2-3%). Tyre-life loss 9-16% (TMC figures) or 15% at 10% under-inflation (Dana). — [Fleet Equipment](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/?p=65440), [CCJ](https://www.ccjdigital.com/business/article/15822374/underinflated-trailer-tires-are-draining-your-fuel-budget). Primary FMCSA report not verified: [FMCSA Tire Condition Sensors study](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130220154813/http://@fmcsa.dot.gov/facts-research/research-technology/report/Commercial-Vehicle-Tire-Condition-Sensors-StudyReport-and-Appendix-A.pdf)
- EPA SmartWay document: less than half of combination-truck tyres within 5% of recommended pressure; only 8% of drivers check with a gauge before each trip. — [EPA 420F16035](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2016-06/documents/420f16035.pdf) (as summarised by search; verify)
- Texaco survey of 500 UK truck fleets: only 42% do regular tyre checks (old, UK). — [Transport Engineer](https://www.transportengineer.org.uk/content/news/pressure-on-to-save-fuel-and-improve-safety)
- Vendor claim (unverified original): IFTRT study says tyre issues cause about 30% of unscheduled downtime. — [Wick Auto blog](https://wickauto.odoo.com/blog/wick-s-tyre-rakhshak-1/unveiling-the-hidden-costs-of-tyre-deflation-in-india-s-trucking-and-logistics-sector-1)

### Inferences
- A defensible planning range for tyre cost share in Indian trucking is roughly 10-30% depending on definition (new purchase vs. including retread/repair) and fuel price. Analysts should not quote a single figure.
- Percent fuel loss from moderate under-inflation is small compared with tyre-life and downtime impact; cost cases built on fuel alone tend to overstate.
- Passenger-vehicle ATMA data is not a truck-fleet compliance rate; use only as evidence that under-inflation is the norm.

### Gaps
- No primary India survey of truck-fleet inflation-check compliance (TMC, Michelin, Bridgestone, Goodyear, CEAT, MRF, Apollo fleet surveys not found).
- Original IFTRT and FMCSA numbers not verified.

## 2. Manual check failures, gauges, hot-tyre inflation, inconsistent targets, outdated spec sheets

### Takeaway
Well-established mechanics: hot readings overstate cold pressure (up to about 15%), stick gauges drift and vary between technicians, and heavy-vehicle pressures (above 100 psi) exceed many gauges' range. No Indian quantitative study on these failure modes was found. The spec-sheet-versus-load/size mismatch point is mostly a logical inference.

### Cited Findings
- Pressure rises with heat; readings taken after driving overstate cold pressure by up to about 15%; check cold. — [Fleet Equipment](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/?p=141461); [Motorist](https://www.motorist.co.th/en/article/5598/tyre-pressure-check-5-mistakes-drivers-often-make)
- Stick gauges should be verified against a master gauge frequently; in a shop with 10 stick gauges readings vary widely unless calibrated. — [Fleet Equipment](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/?p=141461)
- Passenger-vehicle gauges are often inaccurate on commercial tyres above 100 psi. — [Gulf Oil Ltd](https://master.gulfoilltd.com/blog/maintenance-advice/how-to-check-truck-tyre-pressure)
- UK forecourt air lines: over 80% accurate within 5% or 2 psi in a recent check vs about 50% inaccurate in 2005. — [Driving.co.uk](https://www.driving.co.uk/?p=49773)
- Hot-vs-cold comparison creates false alarms and missed events; topping off clears an alert for a day without addressing the leak. — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/fleet-management/tpms-alert-to-fleet-work-order-automated-tire-response) (vendor)
- Apollo data shows 44% of cars over-inflated, i.e. inconsistent targets happen in both directions. — [Tyrepress](https://tyrepress.com/2010/01/apollo-registers-tyre-pressure-safety-concerns-on-indian-market)
- Certified gauge at least monthly and before long trips, with records, is common guidance. — [Gulf Oil Ltd](https://master.gulfoilltd.com/blog/maintenance-advice/how-to-check-truck-tyre-pressure)

### Inferences
- A pressure target should be set by tyre size, ply/load index, axle load and duty (load-inflation tables), not by a single fleet-wide or OEM door-placard number; a spec sheet applied to the wrong size or load will make correct tyres look "under-inflated" or "over-inflated" in data. Analysts should check the spec against the actual fitted tyre and load.
- Data from sensors read in the morning (cold) vs afternoon (hot) will differ by design; compare alerts to a temperature-compensated baseline.

### Gaps
- No Indian source on gauge-calibration compliance rates, or on spec-sheet errors. No authoritative standard found for calibration intervals.

## 3. Rotation, retreading, mixed brands/sizes, replacement logging, lifecycle records

### Takeaway
Retreading is central to Indian truck economics (over 70% of truck tyres retreaded at least once), so casing history matters; record-keeping is largely manual and error-prone. Evidence on mixed brands and rotation compliance is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- More than 70% of truck tyres in India retreaded at least once; retreading market about INR 60 billion (ICRA). — [Tyre Trends/ICRA](https://www.tyre-trends.com/spotlight/70-percent-truck-tyres-in-india-are-retreaded-once-icra)
- Retread cost 30-40% less than new (TRRIB) or 40-50% less (Pulse); NITI Aayog-supported estimate of about Rs 25,000 saving per truck tyre and Rs 15,000 per bus tyre; sector informal and under-incentivised. — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/06/04/why-tyre-retreading-is-crucial-for-economic-environmental-sustainability-in-india.html)
- Cheap imported tyres with weaker casings are less retreadable. — [Tyre Trends](https://tyre-trends.com/spotlight/retreading-reimagined)
- Rotation guidance of every 10,000-12,000 km depending on road/load. — [91trucks](https://www.91trucks.com/news/top-3-ways-to-increase-tyre-life-on-trucks?amp=1)
- A register should hold unique tyre ID, purchase date, position, rotation history; manual records are error-prone; one position-code scheme needed. — [FleetX](https://blog.fleetx.io/top-5-benefits-of-utilizing-the-fleetx-tyre-management-module/); [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/fleet-management/fleet-tire-history-log-template-by-tire-position)
- Brand comparison by cost per km is the standard metric. — [Hanksugi](https://www.hanksugi.com/in/calculator.html) (vendor)

### Inferences
- Replacement, swap or retread events not logged against a vehicle/position make later inflation or wear analysis unreliable (a tyre or sensor may sit on a different vehicle than the register says).
- Mixed sizes/brands on one axle or dual change correct pressure and load sharing; uniform fleet targets will misfire.

### Gaps
- No Indian data on rotation compliance, mixed-brand prevalence or percentage of fleets with tyre registers.

## 4. Driver behaviour, potholes, overloading, roads, monsoon/heat

### Takeaway
Overloading is widespread in India and strains tyres; heat plus under-inflation drives blowouts. Pothole and seasonal fleet-level statistics were not found.

### Cited Findings
- IFTRT estimates 70% of trucks with national permits overload; overloaded tyres burst "quite often"; enforcement is weak. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/trend/death-on-wheels-2197)
- Overloaded vehicles overheat, wear rapidly, risk blowouts. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/bathinda/overloaded-trucks-a-threat-to-commuters-849670)
- Pressure varies greatly in India's summers; under-inflation causes heat build-up and uneven wear. — [91trucks](https://www.91trucks.com/news/top-3-ways-to-increase-tyre-life-on-trucks?amp=1)
- Under-inflation enlarges contact patch, adds friction and heat, leading to bursts. — [Rushlane](https://www.rushlane.com/car-tyre-burst-prevention-12234202.html)
- Tyre failures common on long-haul heavy-load highways (2026 incident report). — [FuelPrice.co.in](https://fuelprice.co.in/news/mumbai-agra-highway-igatpuri-truck-breakdown-freight-fuel-impact-2026)

### Inferences
- Pressure drops tied to a pothole strike or a puncture look identical to "sensor faults" in data; look for step changes on a single wheel vs gradual drift on all wheels.
- Season: ambient-driven pressure swings (hot afternoons, cooler monsoon nights) produce diurnal patterns that are normal.

### Gaps
- No quantitative pothole, monsoon or seasonal data for Indian fleets.

## 5. Process issues: alerts ignored, no follow-up, TPMS ROI vs reality, data-action gap, "sensors are faulty"

### Takeaway
TPMS ROI is real in controlled field tests when alerts trigger maintenance action, but vendor payback claims are modelled. The evidence on ignored alerts is qualitative. Sensor faults, temperature effects and slow leaks are all plausible causes of "bad data"; no study quantifies the split.

### Cited Findings
- US DOT field test of TPMS and automatic tyre inflation: ROI under a year for at least one fleet; FMCSA-reported fuel economy gain 1.4-1.8% with TPMS. — [HDT](https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/dot-study-proves-roi-on-tire-inflation-and-pressure-monitoring-systems); [Trucknews](https://www.trucknews.com/sustainability/tire-pressure-systems-deliver-fuel-savings-extend-tire-life-us-research/1000926162) (secondary; verify against [DOT report](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/178))
- Vendor payback claims of 4-6 months for 50-truck fleet are modelled assumptions. — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/fleet-management/tire-pressure-monitoring-tpms-fuel-efficiency-guide-2026)
- Expert question "can I count on my driver to report and respond to low pressure alerts?"; if not, system may not be worth it. — [HDT, June 2021](https://hdt.mydigitalpublication.com/june-2021/page-56)
- Alert fatigue (20+ alerts per shift claim) is unsourced vendor assertion. — [Motive](https://gomotive.com/blog/cost-of-delayed-action-fleet-management/)
- Sensor faults can arise from loose bead, corrosion, debris on mounting; a sensor fault may mask a real leak; TPMS shouldn't be relied on alone. — [Fleet World](https://fleetworld.co.uk/?p=41419); [TireGrades](https://tiregrades.com/tire-maintenance/pressure/tpms/tire-pressure-sensor-not-reading-correctly/) (low quality)
- India TPMS: AIS-151 mandate for medium/heavy vehicles claimed by market-research firms, not confirmed from an official source. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-aftermarket-tpms-market)
- Indian TPMS vendors/launches: Vamosys, JK Tyre TREEL, Fleeca, Rivigo-JK Tyre. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/vamosys-launches-indias-first-gps-based-tpms-solution-for-trucking-enterprises/985447); [Fleeca](https://tyres.cardekho.com/news/fleeca-india-launches-tyre-pressure-monitoring-system-devices-for-heavy-commercial-vehicles)

### Inferences
- ROI depends on the alert-to-work-order loop; a deployment with sensors but no action reproduces baseline costs.
- "Sensors are faulty" may be true for some units, but check for alternative causes first.

### Gaps
- No Indian TPMS case study with measured outcomes; no data on alert response rates or on the share of alerts that are sensor faults vs leaks.

## 6. Misleading client narratives and triangulation (analyst guidance, mostly inference)

### Takeaway
No source catalogues misleading client narratives; the following are inferences from the cited evidence, to be tested against the data.

### Cited Findings
- (Supporting facts only) Hot vs cold readings, top-ups masking leaks, and gauge drift all generate data that can be misread as sensor error — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/fleet-management/tpms-alert-to-fleet-work-order-automated-tire-response); [Fleet Equipment](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/?p=141461).

### Inferences
- "Sensors are faulty": check whether the low readings are (a) confined to specific wheel positions or vehicles (leak, valve, rim) versus random; (b) step-wise recoveries after top-ups (manual inflation, so real pressure loss); (c) correlated with ambient or time of day (thermal); (d) flat-lined or missing (true sensor/comms fault); (e) consistent with the spec target applied (wrong target).
- "We check pressures daily": compare against logged inflation events and the recovery pattern in data.
- "Drivers are the problem": test whether alerts resulted in pressure recovery within a defined time; distinguish no-response from no-capability (no air access).
- "Spec sheet is right": verify the target against actual tyre size, load index and axle load.
- "TPMS saved us X%": check against baseline period, route and load changes and season.
- "The tyre is under-inflated" may be a spec error: if a whole vehicle sits steadily below target with no recovery, suspect the wrong target before suspecting leaks.

### Gaps
- No published taxonomy of client narratives; all items are analyst inference.
